Historical Exhibition of Sunagawa

Ameblo · 2022年8月13日 · Original page / 元ページ ·

Historical Exhibition in Community Center "Sunagawa Gakushuukan" 

Many of the meetings, gatherings and other events organized by Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba took place in a 1400 square feet auditorium of Sunagawa Gakushuukan (a learning center in the Sunagawa district). The two-story building was originally constructed in 1973 on the site where the town office of Sunagawa had existed until the town was incorporated into Tachikawa City in 1963.
Sunagawa Gakushuukan was so named in 2007 when the building was redefined as a community center to serve the local residents for lifelong learning and child-rearing. It also contained a permanent exhibition of photos and goods which were relevant to the regional history, including the Sunagawa Toso, in a part of its entrance hall.
But with the number of children decreasing and the ratio of elderly citizens increasing, Tachikawa City has been planning to streamline the municipal schools and existing community centers. Its plan includes the scrapping and rebuilding of Sunagawa Gakushuukan to promote multi-generational interchange in the local community. As a result, the new learning center in Sunagawa is likely to be smaller in size (a 20% reduction in a total floor area) and more multi-functional, placing relatively less emphasis on a historical exhibition.
Over the past one or two years, the official plan and concerns about it were heard on various occasions such as small public hearings conducted by the city officials as well as question-and-answer sessions in city assembly. 
Some members of Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba have been personaly involved in the above-mentioned process as local users of Sunagawa Gakushuukan.

Arguments in City Assembly of Tachikawa

image from HP of Tachikawa City

For instance, in the city assembly held in September 2021, one of its members KIHARA raised the exhibition issue. He said that some neighbors feel uncomfortable and reluctant to see “things with a political color” in place in Sunagawa Gakushuukan, suggesting the removal of such things upon the renewal of the building.
Kihara himself is aware of the significance of “the Sunagawa Toso as part of the major history of Sunagawa” and regards it as an important subject to consider how the history of Sunagawa district should be handed down to the next generation, as was recorded in the minutes of the assembly session.
In December, 2021, at a regular meeting of the city assembly, another member WAKAKI raised a question to confirm that the exhibition be maintained but the head of the city’s education department answered that, due to a 20% reduction in floor area, there would not be any more permanent exhibition space dedicated to any subject, and walls instead should accommodate exhibitions of art and other works as necessary.

Requests from Residents and Users in Sunagawa District
In response, Sunagawa Machizukuri Kyogikai , a town planning council, composed of city assembly members, users of Sunagawa Gakushuukan and local residents, together with members of Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba, voiced their complaints, requesting more understandable explanations from the municipal authorities.
On March 19, 2022, when an explanatory meeting was held in Gakushuukan, the head of education clearly stated that a permanent exhibition of the history of Sunagawa be maintained on the wall and mentioned a peace museum as well.
Machizukuri Kyogikai submitted questions about the possible elimination of the historical exhibition and formed a liaison to retain the exhibition space for the history and culture of Sunagawa. FUKUSHIMA Kyoko of Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba is one of the joint representatives of the liaison.
The liaison submitted a formal request to the Mayor SHIMIZU of Tachikawa on June 29, 2022, and had a press conference on the same day.
Their request includes:
１，To maintain a permanent and substantial exhibition of materials concerning the history and culture of Sunagawa area,
２， To include actual goods in exhibition to help viewers deepen their understanding of history and culture [indicating things like real farm tools and a traditional fire bell, which was used for warning farmers and supporters against the arrival of the land survey team during the period of Sunagawa Toso].
In their request, the liaison elaborated on the reasoning behind the existing exhibition as well as its future implications. 
The exhibition has been presented as part of Sunagawa Gkushukan on the homepage of Tachikawa City since it was established based on the city’s town planning policy to promote collaboration between citizens and administration. Both parties agreed in 2009 on the basic direction toward a peaceful utilization of the land with the history of the Sunagawa Toso taken into consideration. In addition, a plan to construct the Peace Memorial Hall, as it was tentatively called, was proposed.


The exhibition has offered valuable local information and is expected to play an even more important role in the future as the 4th long-term comprehensive program of the city aims to strengthen the function of Gakushuukan in each district to better support local citizens in solving their specific problems. 
Starting from FY2022, a new subject called Tachikawa citizenship is to be added to the curriculum of elementary and junior high schools. Children of Tachikawa will learn how to play a part in creating a peaceful and democratic society through their real life and studies based on their local identity.
