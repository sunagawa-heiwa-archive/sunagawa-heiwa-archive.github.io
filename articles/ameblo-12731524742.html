About Sunagawa

Ameblo · 2022年3月12日 · Original page / 元ページ

 To Begin with

This year marks the 65th anniversary of the Sunagawa Incident. In fact, 23 protestors were arrested in September, 1957, and seven of them prosecuted on a charge of violating the second Article of the Special Criminal Act attendant upon the Administrative Agreement based on Japan-US Security Treaty.

Commemorating the incident and with an aim to express our support for the plaintiffs who are still fighting against unlawful measures taken by Japan’s political and judicial leaders in those days, we will present a series of English articles as follows:

(1) About Suna-gawa 砂川

(2) About Sunagawa Toso (struggle) 砂川闘争

(3) About Court Battles and “Date Judgment” 伊達判決

Here is the very first part to begin with:

(1) About Suna-gawa 砂川

Sunagawa, which literally means “sand river,” was a farming village so named in 1736. Located in the Musashino Plain to the west of central Edo, the area had been developed by local farmers under the ruling of the Tokugawa Government. With water supplies and reclaimed land increasing, Sunagawa kept expanding until 1893 when it was reorganized by the Meiji Government as Sunagawa Village in Tokyo as well as Tachikawa Village adjacent to it.

But as Japanese imperialism escalated in the 20th century, land of both villages was often requisitioned for military purposes; Japanese Imperial army’s flight units were stationed. Tachikawa Airfield was constructed in 1922 and related facilities were established to serve the military base. 

As a result, the area’s economy boosted with population growth to make Sunagawa a target of American air raids during the Asian-Pacific War. According to the regional history, more than one hundred B29 bombers attacked Sunagawa on April 24th , 1945, and at least 134 villagers, including three local school teachers, were killed.

At the end of war on August 15th of the same year, the Sunagawa land previously confiscated by the Japanese military was not returned for cultivation, because the base was taken over by the US military and Japan was under the occupation of General Headquarters (GHQ) headed by General MacArthur. 

Even after Japan regained its independence with signing of the San Francisco Peace Treaty in September, 1951, US Forces remained. The Treaty was concluded as separate peace between the two countries and was coupled with US-Japan Security Treaty, which permitted the U.S. to continuing military base operations in Japan.

When the Korean War broke out in 1950, US Tachikawa Air Base served as the largest staging grounds in the Far East. Tachikawa, which had been designated as a city in 1940 and whose population exceeded 60,000 in 1950, became prosperous. Military personnel spent freely in Tachikawa, in part, because of its location directly outside of the main gate..

 On the other hand, Sunagawa, located at the north end of the runway, suffered from noise pollution and water pollution. A plane crash in 1953 reminded residents of the threat that the base posed to their health and well-being. 

Then suddenly in May 1955, the newly elected mayor of Sunagawa received news that the runway of Tachikawa Air Base was to be expanded northwards, cutting through the community. The vitality and future of the place, which had grown from a village to a town, in 1954 was threatened by the planned construction. 

The residents whose land was to be expropriated formed an alliance to fight against the base expansion immediately upon hearing the news. The mayor, MIYAZAKI Denzaemon and the town assembly also expressed opposition to the plan. 

Nonetheless, the Procurement Bureau of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government began to survey the farmland as early as June, 1955.
