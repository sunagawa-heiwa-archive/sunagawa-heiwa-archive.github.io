About Our Activities

Ameblo · 2022年5月9日 · Original page / 元ページ

  Three Pillars of Our Activities

Since the gathering held in the fall of 2015, commemorating the 60th anniversary of the Sunagawa Toso, those who organized and supported the event came back to meet regularly. We discussed what we could do to maintain and evolve Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba both physically and strategically.

As a result of our continued efforts over several years, we reorganized ourselves as active members of Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba and identified the three pillars of our activities as follows:

(1) to collect and archive materials related to the history of Sunagawa and the Sunagawa Toso for a permanent exhibition as well as for our own research purposes.

(2) to plan and organize various events commemorating the Sunagawa Toso and/or advocating peace actions.

(3) to provide a community space for local citizens to drop in and for children/mothers/senior citizens/ or anybody in need to find support.

(1) Research and Archiving

Our stock of materials include historical photos and maps but a major part of our collection is composed of court documents and personal writings left by MIYAOKA Masao, an original founder of Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba as well as a sub-captain of the Sunagawa Action Corps of the anti-base alliance in the 1950s.

For example, a handwritten diary that he kept while stationed in Taiwan as soldier in Japan’s Imperial Army and his note about his personal history help us understand what he learned from the absurdity of his wartime experience and how he endured a long battle at court based on his post-war awareness of human rights under the new Constitution.

(2) Field Works, Meetings and Other Events

We have also established an annual routine of proposing a field work or a study tour in and around Sunagawa in the spring/summer and organizing a lecture/film-viewing meeting in fall. We were able to share those opportunities with a wide variety of people ranging from old activists to young students. 

It has turned out that all of us have quite a few things in common to discuss on a continued basis, despite the fact that we have come together from different backgrounds, from various parts of not only Japan but also the world.

Since the outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic, however, we felt obliged to modify the forms of our activities. So, we tried an open-air lecture with a limited number of audience members and distributed the recorded video on the internet and in a form of DVD.

We also initiated a series of citizens’ seminar to learn about Sunagawa and discuss the problems of military bases from the perspectives of local environmental issues as well as peace and security matters in East Asia. We have been carrying out the lecture and discussion through a zoom meeting so interested people can participate from anywhere.

(3) Community-based Programs

In corporation with Tachikawa Council of Social Welfare, we offer free meals and free school programs. 

Usually, Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba is open from 12:00 to 14:00 on the first Sunday of each month, to provide free meals for children. Adults can join, too, but are expected to put a few coins into the okimochi box just as they like (“okimochi” is a polite form of a Japanese which means your sentiment/feeling).

Curry & rice and Japanese noodle udon are the most popular menus and they mostly use fresh vegetables or wheat cultivated in the fields just behind Sunagawa Heiwa Hiroba. Due to our limited capacity, we can accept only 20 persons to sit and eat every month.

Again, because of the Covid-19 pandemic, this program became unavailable or sometimes changed to take-out service from the “pantry,” which provides canned food, frozen food, snacks and other types of food stuff as well as soft drinks and yogurt.

The free school program is offered from 14:30 to 16:00, but it can be more flexible to meet the needs and convenience of children who are interested in attending.

Participants are always welcome to register prior to participation using the LINE application.
