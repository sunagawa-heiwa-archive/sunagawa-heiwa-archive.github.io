(3)　About Court Battles and "Date Judgment" 

伊達判決

Since the moment he perceived the possible plundering of farmland, MIYAOKA Masao was determined not to give any more properties up to the nation. He had sacrificed himself for his nation’s wartime ambition and suffered the loss of his house during the 1945 firebombing raid. Under the imperial militarism, people of Sunagawa had obediently submitted their own land for the national cause of war.

And yet, as he gradually learned in the post-war enlightenment, the war turned out to be an unreasonable challenge to the Western Powers and an unjustifiable invasion to Asian countries. He also became aware of individual’s basic rights guaranteed by the new Constitution. Keeping Roppouzensho, a thick volume of Japanese six codes, always at hand, he worked hard on civil and criminal and administrative lawsuits while serving as sub-captain of Sunagawa Action Corps.

MIYAOKA Masao and his Roppozensho composed of the Constitution and five major codes: photographed by TAKASE Tsuyoshi 高瀬剛

砂川闘争の現場を歩く――土地はなぜ返還されたのか - Yahoo!ニュース

One of the most remarkable outcomes of those cases was what has been legendarily called the “Date Judgment.”

Even after the anti-base alliance in Sunagawa successfully blocked surveyors from measuring their land on site, the Procurement Office of Tokyo persisted in its assessment from inside the US Air Base fence line. The survey included land owned by local farmers and leased to the US, even though the landowners refused to renew the lease and demanded that their land to be returned to them.

 In June and July 1957, active protests against the unjust land measurement were periodically revitalized. On July 8th, for instance, some demonstrators penetrated the base perimeter. It was an incidental act and they were simply made to retreat by police at the time. 

Armed US guards had observed the incident and it was photographed by Japanese police. Nonetheless, nothing serious came of the incident for more than two months following. 

Then suddenly on September 22nd, police arrested twenty-three workers and students at different locations around Tokyo simultaneously.

Seven people were prosecuted on a charge of violating the second Article of the Special Criminal Act attendant upon the Administrative Agreement based on Japan-US Security Treaty. Public trials of the Sunagawa Incident case were held at the District Court of Tachikawa. 

On March 30th, 1959, the presiding judge DATE Akio declared all the defendants not guilty on the ground that the presence of the US military base did not comply with the Constitution of Japan. 

The prosecution instantly appealed to the Supreme Court, skipping the due process of the High Court. 

Date Judgment was reversed by TANAKA Kotaro, chief justice of the Supreme Court, and the case was returned to the District Court. In the end, the defendants accepted the unfavorable re-judgment and paid small fines to settle the case.

Nearly half a century later, a Japanese journalist and a scholar separately discovered previously classified documents released by the US National Archive. Those official documents revealed evidence of American intervention in the Japanese judiciary and a clandestine contact between TANAKA and Douglas MacArthur, the nephew of former General MacArthur and American Ambassador to Japan since 1959.

For instance, the Mainichi (morning paper dated 04/30/2008) reported that TANAKA allegedly met the US Ambassador in secret to inform him of the ongoing Sunagawa case before he made his ruling on it. The Ambassador’s telegram to the US Secretary of State also suggested that the Ambassador had visited the Japanese Minister of Foreign Affairs immediately after the Date Judgment to recommend the case to be jumped to the Supreme Court. 

Obviously both American and Japanese political leaders expected a judiciary conclusion invalidating the Date Judgement to be announced timely enough for the revision of the Security Treaty, scheduled for June 1960, to proceed without problem.

Having learned that their criminal case was affected by unlawful measures, the surviving defendants of the Sunagawa Incident demanded state compensation and official apologies in March, 2019, claiming that their right to a trial by an impartial tribunal specified by Article 37 of the Japanese Constitution was violated by TANAKA. 

As of March, 2022, they are still fighting at the Tokyo District Court against the politically corrupt practices in the judiciary.

(The End)
